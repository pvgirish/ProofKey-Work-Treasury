How ProofKey does it

ProofKey is the application you use. Its screen tracks the work; its contracts are on-chain rules that enforce the agreed payments. It uses Ethereum and Creditcoin rather than operating either network.

1

Ethereum

A shared computer network that records transactions and runs rules (a blockchain), not a bank. It records the outcome under the agreed work rules.

2

Attestcoin

Creditcoin's verification system checks evidence of the outcome recorded on Ethereum. It does not judge the work. This is network infrastructure, not a service operated by ProofKey.

3

Creditcoin

A shared computer network that records transactions and runs rules (a blockchain), not a bank. It holds the existing budget and sends allocated tokens from that budget to the agreed payment wallet (withdrawal)—not cash or a bank payout.

Test CTC is used on Creditcoin's test network—not rupees or dollars. The transactions recorded here occurred on test networks; the budget and payments use test CTC.

The approval evidence crosses networks. The work budget does not: it stays on Creditcoin.

This fits an existing setup: keep your Ethereum Safe approval process while using a budget already on Creditcoin.

Read the longer design explanation

Why two networks are used here. A team can keep its existing Ethereum approval process while using a budget already on Creditcoin. Evidence crosses networks; the budget does not.

How the outcome is recognized. Attestcoin supplies authenticated cross-network evidence. The route recognizes the outcome only after native verification and target recognition. People and their job policy still assess the work.

What the currency is. CTC is Creditcoin's native token; this demonstration uses only test-network practice tokens.

What the infrastructure is not. Attestcoin is part of Creditcoin's network infrastructure, not a ProofKey-operated work-review service. Creditcoin describes its attestor network in its official explanation.

Proof or operator availability can delay collection. Outcomes include review, dispute and timeout rules; they are not automatic satisfaction guarantees. This testnet prototype does not provide a production payment guarantee.

Withdrawn means sent from the work budget to the recipient's payment wallet—not converted into cash or transferred to a bank. This prototype has no bank payout or INR conversion.

Recorded testnet · Story step 1 of 4 · The agreement

Agree the jobs. Set aside their payments.

Test CTC. This example uses test CTC, a practice token used on Creditcoin's test network—not rupees or dollars. Network fees are separate.

In this recorded test, the team funded a work budget of 120 test CTC. The first job reserved 30 and the second reserved 40. The remaining 50 was not assigned to a job.

Meaning
Reserved means committed to an unresolved job. It does not mean the worker has been paid.
Roles
Team: agrees the terms and funds the program. Worker: agrees the work and payment rules.
Limit
The rules include review, dispute and timeout outcomes. ProofKey does not promise payment only when the buyer is satisfied.

Disagreement? See who decides.

Recorded testnet · Story step 2 of 4 · Approval is not payment

The first job was approved. Payment still had to be collected.

30 test CTC owed for the first job — not yet withdrawn at this stage.

The recorded outcome allocated 30 test CTC for the first job. Attestcoin authentication and Creditcoin recognition came before withdrawal. Withdrawn means sent from the work budget to the recipient's payment wallet—not converted into cash or transferred to a bank. This prototype has no bank payout or INR conversion. An approved amount is not a payment receipt.

Who submits, pays gas, and collects

Who can submit: the team, the worker, or another submitter. They need usable evidence and network gas. Services responding and someone acting are not guaranteed.

Where withdrawal goes: once an allocation is recognized, anyone can send its public withdrawal to the agreed payment wallet. A submitter cannot replace that destination through the public withdrawal. Only the claim owner has a separate redirect path.

Who pays each network fee: the account sending each transaction pays its network fee. If the worker sends the withdrawal, the worker pays; if the team or another submitter sends it, that sender pays. There is no automatic team subsidy, and receiving alone does not incur the sender's gas charge.

Related budget action
The 50 not assigned to a job was released from the program and credited to the funding account after verification. It had not yet been withdrawn to that account's wallet.

Recorded testnet · Story step 3 of 4 · Agreed changes and follow-up

Both sides agreed 25 for the second job. The unused 15 supported another job.

The second job had reserved 40 test CTC. A mutual settlement allocated 25 and released 15 for further work. This was not a unilateral reduction by the buyer.

Follow-up
That released 15 funded a follow-up job. Its recorded no-delivery outcome allocated no work payment, making the 15 available again.
Meaning
Money released by a job can support later work while the program is open. Money already retired from the program is not reused inside it.

Disagreement? See who decides.

Illustration of existing rules—not another recorded transaction

If the team and worker disagree

Three committee members are named in the accepted terms. Two must agree on the same award. Naming them does not guarantee impartiality.

Illustrative accepted terms

One illustrative job reserves 10 test CTC. Its job-policy fee is 0 and its pre-agreed challenged-timeout payment is 2. Two distinct named members must submit valid matching votes for the same award.

These cutoffs are illustrative and job-specific source-block numbers, not promised wall-clock hours:

Accept
before source block 1,000,050
Deliver
before source block 1,000,100
Challenge or review
before source block 1,000,200
Ruling
before source block 1,000,300
  1. Terms agreed, 10 reserved. Fee 0 and fallback 2 are disclosed up front.
  2. Work delivered and challenged. The worker delivers before source block 1,000,100 and asks for 10; the team challenges before source block 1,000,200. Verification does not decide who is right.

Ruling cutoff · before source block 1,000,300 — only one branch can occur:

Committee outcome

Two matching votes award 6

Before source block 1,000,300, two named committee members' valid matching votes for 6 are submitted. The second vote records the outcome.

10 = 6 owed + 4 restored

The 4 returns to available program capacity. It is not yet refunded to a bank or wallet.

Timeout outcome

No ruling by the cutoff

If still challenged at or after source block 1,000,300, anyone can submit the timeout transaction.

10 = 2 owed + 8 restored

No transaction means no automatic outcome or payment. The restored 8 stays in available program capacity.

Either owed amount still requires proof, recognition, and withdrawal before it is received. The team and worker may instead mutually settle. The first valid terminal result wins.

These are example terms, not another recorded payment. Block cutoffs track Ethereum's progress, not promised clock times; reaching a cutoff does not send money.
